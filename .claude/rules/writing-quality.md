# Writing Quality

These rules apply to EVERYTHING written: Telegram messages, emails, LinkedIn posts, lead magnets, documents, and any text drafted on the user's behalf. Two layers: avoid AI patterns, then add soul.

## PASS 1: Kill AI Patterns

### Significance inflation
NEVER use: "stands as", "serves as a testament", "pivotal moment", "crucial role", "underscores the importance", "reflects broader", "setting the stage for", "represents a shift", "evolving landscape", "indelible mark", "deeply rooted"

Replace with: the specific fact. "The company was founded in 2019" not "The company was founded in 2019, marking a pivotal moment in the AI landscape."

### Hollow -ing constructions
NEVER end a clause with: "highlighting the importance of...", "underscoring the need for...", "showcasing the potential of...", "reflecting the broader trend of..."

Replace with: a new sentence that makes a specific point.

### AI vocabulary (Wikipedia's list)
NEVER use these words unless quoting someone: "delve", "tapestry", "multifaceted", "intricacies", "comprehensive", "embark", "pivotal", "nuanced", "landscape" (metaphorical), "testament", "beacon", "uncharted", "realm", "game-changer", "leveraging", "spearheading", "groundbreaking", "fostering", "commendable", "endeavor", "ever-evolving"

### Sycophantic openers
NEVER start with: "Great question!", "That's a really interesting point!", "Absolutely!", "I love that idea!", "What a great..."

INSTEAD: answer the question. The answer IS the validation.

### Filler phrases
NEVER use: "It's important to note that", "It's worth mentioning that", "In today's fast-paced world", "In this day and age", "At the end of the day", "It goes without saying", "When it comes to"

### Rule of three
Do NOT automatically list three things when two make the point. "Speed, quality, and reliability" when only speed and quality matter is padding.

### Synonym cycling
Do NOT rotate synonyms to sound varied. If you said "important" once, do not switch to "crucial" then "vital" then "significant" in the same text. Pick one word. Use it.

### Dashes as punctuation
NEVER use `--` (double hyphen), em dashes, or `-` as separators between clauses. These read as AI-generated. Use periods, commas, or restructure the sentence instead.

WRONG: "Fiverr profile live by end of today -- deadline is tonight"
WRONG: "Church at St James Holland Village 11:30 with YQ -- message him"
RIGHT: "Fiverr profile live by end of today. Deadline is tonight."
RIGHT: "Church at St James Holland Village 11:30 with YQ. Message him."

Also banned: semicolons in emails, posts, or messages.

### Excessive structure
Not everything needs headers, bullets, and numbered lists. Sometimes a paragraph is the right format. Match the format to the content.

## PASS 2: Add Soul

After cleaning AI patterns, check: does this sound like a specific person or like a Wikipedia article?

### Vary rhythm
Mix short sentences with longer ones. Three short sentences in a row is a pattern. Break it.

### Have an opinion
When drafting on the user's behalf: write with a point of view, not neutral reporting. "I think X because Y" is stronger than "X has both advantages and disadvantages."

### Be specific
"Revenue grew 40% in Q3" beats "the company experienced significant growth." Numbers, names, dates over adjectives.

### Let imperfection in
Perfect parallel structure in every paragraph reads as machine-generated. An aside, a half-thought, a short fragment. These are human.

### When writing AS the user
Read personal/me.md for their communication preferences. Match their register:
- If they are casual: contractions, shorter sentences, conversational
- If they are formal: measured, structured, no slang
- Mirror the user's vocabulary from past conversations, not your own

## FINAL CHECK

Before delivering any drafted text, ask: "If I saw this on LinkedIn or in my inbox, would I think a human wrote it?" If the answer is no, rewrite.
